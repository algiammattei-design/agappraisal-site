The thing worth knowing

Two appraisers can value the same house differently and both be right.

People find that hard to believe, and it is the single most useful thing to understand before you order anything. An appraisal is not a fact about a house the way its square footage is a fact. It is an opinion of value, on a specific date, for a specific use. Change the date and the number changes with it.

That sounds like a technicality. It is not. Someone whose mother passed in early 2023 needs the house valued as it was on the day she died, because that is what sets the stepped up basis the heirs inherit. Value it as of today instead and the number could be tens of thousands off, and the report will not do the job it was bought for.

The date is not paperwork. It is part of the answer.

Divorce work is where this bites hardest. Florida does not fix one date for equitable distribution. Depending on the case it might be the date of filing, the date of separation, or the date of trial, and in a market that has moved the way Central Florida's has, those can be very different numbers. The attorney names the date. If nobody has named one before the appraisal is ordered, that is the first phone call to make, not the last.

Property tax appeals have the same trap with a different date. Florida assesses every property as of January 1. Not the day your TRIM notice landed in August, not the day you decided to fight it. A report dated today is answering a question the Value Adjustment Board did not ask, and that is where most homeowner appeals quietly fall apart.

None of this is difficult once someone tells you. Nobody tells you. So here is the whole thing on one page, and if your situation is not on it, call and ask. It takes about four minutes and it costs nothing.

The Florida calendar

Four dates that decide your property tax bill.

These are statutory and they do not move for anybody. The August one is the one people miss, and missing it means waiting a full year.

January 1

Florida assesses your property's just value as of this date. Everything that happens later in the year is an argument about this one morning.

March 1

Deadline to file for homestead exemption, and to file for portability if you have moved. Save Our Homes caps assessed increases on a homesteaded property at 3% or CPI, whichever is lower, and portability carries up to $500,000 of that benefit to your next Florida homestead.

Mid August

TRIM notices go in the mail. This is the proposed tax bill, not the real one, and it tells you what the county thinks your house was worth on January 1.

25 days later

The hard deadline. A petition to the Value Adjustment Board has to be received within 25 days of the mailing date, usually mid September. A postmark does not count. The exact date is printed on your notice. You can ask the property appraiser for an informal review first, but the petition still has to be in within the same 25 days.

An independent appraisal dated January 1 is the evidence a hearing is built on. It is not required and it does not guarantee a reduction. If the county's number looks about right to me, I will say so on the phone and save you the fee. More on tax appeal appraisals.

Investors and builders

Two numbers decide a deal, and they carry different dates.

As is tells you what you are buying. As repaired tells you what you are building. The gap between them, less your scope of work, is the whole deal. Both ends are worth more coming from somebody with no stake in whether you close.

Buyers · lenders · partners

As is value

As of today

What the property is worth today in the condition it is actually in, with nothing repaired and nothing assumed. This is the figure a lender, a partner or a hard money source asks for before anybody funds anything, and it is the one people most often try to skip.

Flips · rehab · rental conversions

As repaired value

As of today, subject to completion

What it is worth once your scope of work is finished, written subject to completion against your plans and your budget. The only hypothetical part is that the work has not happened yet. Everything underneath it is closed sales, adjusted the same way any other report is adjusted. On a flip this is the number the whole thing turns on, which is exactly why it should not be a guess.

Builders · construction lenders

New and under construction

From plans, or at a stage of completion

Appraised from plans and specifications before the slab is poured, or as of a stage of completion partway through the build. Construction lenders draw against these, so they get written to be drawn against.

News and notes

What is actually happening right now.

Short notes on the market and the tax calendar, written for somebody who is about to order something and wants to know whether the timing matters. Updated as things move.

Property tax · August 2026

Amendment 3 goes to the ballot on 3 November

Florida votes on a homestead exemption increase. If sixty percent say yes, a qualifying homestead gets up to $150,000 of assessed value exempted in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. It takes effect on 1 January 2027 and first appears on the August 2027 TRIM notice and the November 2027 bill. School taxes and non ad valorem assessments are not included. Be clear about what it does not do: it moves the exemption, not the value of the house. Arguing the assessed value itself is still an appraisal dated January 1.

Source: Pinellas County Property Appraiser, Amendment 3 FAQ.

Market · August 2026

Orlando has come off its peak, which matters most looking backward

Median prices around Orlando are running a few percent below peak, inventory is up sharply on last year, and houses are sitting closer to two months than two weeks. None of that changes a retrospective appraisal. A date of death in 2023, or a tax appeal dated 1 January, is measured against the market on that day and not against this one. The distance between those two markets is exactly why the effective date is the first question I ask.

Source: Orlando Regional REALTOR Association market narrative.

Property tax · For next year

Missed this year's TRIM window? Here is what still helps

The 2026 petition window closed in September. A late petition is accepted only for good cause, which the Value Adjustment Board decides case by case, so do not count on one. If you think your assessment is high, the next chance is the August 2027 TRIM notice. That appeal will again be argued about one date: January 1, 2027. An appraisal dated close to that day is the strongest evidence a hearing can use, and it is easier to support when it is ordered near the date than reconstructed later. If you filed a petition this year and your hearing is still ahead, call and ask about an appraisal dated January 1, 2026.

Source: Florida Statutes, VAB petition window.

Selling · Still catching people out

What changed for sellers in August 2024, and what did not

An offer of compensation to a buyer's agent can no longer be published on the MLS. It is negotiated separately now, off the listing, and whether you make one at all is your decision rather than a rule. What did not change: commissions were always negotiable and still are, and a buyer still pays what the evidence supports rather than what the commission structure suggests.

Source: National Association of REALTORS practice changes.

Questions

The ones that come up on the phone.

How is this different from what my realtor gave me?

A realtor's comparative market analysis is a pricing opinion, usually free, from someone paid on the sale. An appraisal is a formal opinion of value from a state licensed appraiser, written to a standard, with no interest in the outcome. Courts, the IRS and lenders accept one of them.

Do I need to clean the house first?

No. It is not being shown and it is not going on a website. Clutter does not change what a house is worth. What helps is access. Leave the gate open, the shed open, the attic hatch reachable and any sticking door unlatched, so nothing has to be estimated or excluded from the report.

Can you value a house as of a date in the past?

Yes, routinely. It is called a retrospective appraisal. The sales that closed around that date still exist, so the market as of that day can be reconstructed properly. Nearly all estate work is retrospective and some divorce work is too.

How is the number actually arrived at?

Recent sales of comparable properties, adjusted for the ways they differ from yours: gross living area, lot, age, condition, layout, garage, pool, view, and how the market moved between their sale date and your effective date. The adjustments are shown in the report so you can follow the reasoning instead of taking a number on faith.

The county says my house is worth more than I think. Can you help?

Sometimes. Read the TRIM notice carefully first, because the proposed tax and the assessed value are different numbers and people often fight the wrong one. The appraisal has to be dated January 1 of that tax year, and the petition window is 25 days from the mailing date with no late filing. An appraisal does not guarantee a reduction. If the county's figure looks about right, I will tell you and save you the fee.

Can one appraisal be used for two things?

Usually not. A report names its intended use and its intended users, and using it for something else is outside what it was written for. If you need the same property valued for a refinance and for an estate, say so up front and it can be handled properly.

How long does it take?

Typically 48 hours from the inspection for standard residential work. Complex properties, acreage or thin comparable data take longer, and you will hear that from me before you commit rather than after.

Can I order one on a property I do not own?

Attorneys, personal representatives, trustees and lenders do it all the time. What matters is lawful access to the property and a clear engagement naming the intended users. If you are unsure, ask, because checking is quicker than redoing.
